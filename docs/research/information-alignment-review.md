**Ruling: MODIFY, then accept as an explicitly versioned experimental entry rule.** Freeze the original selected-side book timestamp, require an actually newer spot observation to reach it, cancel on every observed invalidation, and recompute the complete current decision before reservation. ## Precise proposed semantics

Call an episode one eligible candidate followed until confirmation or cancellation. An episode is diagnostic state, not an opening intent, cash reservation, or execution authorization. Keep the ordinary stateless `value` decision alongside the gate outcome so unconfirmed eligibility remains observable.

Let the original episode contain selected side `a`, spot source time `s0`, selected-side book source time `b0`, source/receive/decision times and record identifiers, round/condition/token identity, reference identity/value, and configuration/rule version. The candidate watermark is the fixed integer-millisecond value `b0`.

1. **Arm.** Only for the candidate configured for execution (`value` or modified `momentum`), when its current complete decision is `ENTRY` and ordinary entry/account/round controls permit considering an entry, create a pending episode. Do not reserve, sign, or submit. Do not overwrite `b0` with later book timestamps while that episode survives. Moving the watermark each snapshot can make confirmation impossible under a persistent feed lag.
2. **Observe and cancel before testing confirmation.** On every subsequent available snapshot, run all existing validity and strategy checks. Cancel if the current decision is not `ENTRY` for the same selected side, including a failed price band, spread, depth, surplus, data age/history, rule/reference, active-market, or entry-window check. A missing snapshot, adapter failure/disconnect, halt/stop, unresolved account state, or competing persisted opening intent also cancels pending entry state. A round/condition/token/configuration or material reference/rule change starts a different episode. Ordinary passage within the 60–180 second window does not itself reset the episode. An authoritative anchor confirmation at the same validated value can retain it while recording upgraded provenance; a conflict cannot.
3. **Require actual progress.** Confirmation must use a later snapshot/evaluation and a spot with `s1 > s0` **and** `s1 >= b0`. Replaying the original snapshot, a duplicate spot, an RTDS envelope timestamp, a TWAP point, or a later local receive timestamp cannot satisfy spot progress. Reordered older points cannot move the latest spot backward. Retain exact milliseconds and normal timestamp-validity checks; do not add the one-second future tolerance to the watermark comparison. Requiring `s1 > s0` is a small explicit strengthening of the proposal: it prevents a reused spot from confirming candidates whose original spot already equalled/exceeded `b0`. In that case the extra wait is a persistence experiment, not a necessary correction of an original timestamp deficit.
4. **Confirm once using current economics.** At the first surviving snapshot that meets those conditions, require the full freshly computed selected-side `ENTRY` decision. Use its current books, model time, history, fees, depth, signed limit and amounts. Do not execute the original limit/size merely because the old candidate was eligible. Neither the same price nor the same book hash is required. An opposite-side decision cancels the original episode and may only arm a new one with its own watermark; it cannot confirm the old side.
5. **Consume safely.** Clear/consume the pending episode when handing the fresh decision to ordinary reservation and execution. Existing risk checks, one-opening-intent-per-round uniqueness, fresh metadata checks, persist-before-POST, unknown-order freezes, and the final time/data checks still apply. Confirmation grants no right to use a stale decision after an awaited operation. Material signing metadata changes require re-evaluation as already designed; they do not silently preserve the old approved economics. A rejected reservation does not cause repeated attempts from the same confirmation record; later eligibility must arm a new episode.
6. **Restart and re-arm.** Discard pending confirmations on restart or an interrupted observation interval and record that reason. Recover real persisted intents through the existing ledger; never infer an executable confirmation from historical diagnostic events. A canceled episode does not burn the round's opening-intent allowance. Later eligibility can arm a new episode, including the same side, but it gets a new identity and watermark and cannot confirm on its arming snapshot. Preserve every canceled episode in reports rather than replacing it with the later survivor.

Eligibility is required at each *observed* intervening snapshot. No claim is made that it held continuously between observations. Current mutable fee/tick changes must at least rerun the complete ordinary checks; if the execution path invalidates its decision for such a change, confirmation must be obtained again rather than carrying forward a prepared intent.

Minimum diagnostic record: episode ID; original/current decision IDs; source observation and book IDs or deterministic content hashes where no native IDs exist; side/token/round/condition; original/current source and receive timestamps; decision timestamps; reference/configuration identities; original/current eligibility and surplus; transition/reason; wait elapsed time; `b0-s0`; `b0-s1`; and `b1-s1`, where `b1` is the current selected book's timestamp. Signed differences preserve whether spot leads or trails. Use monotonic time for elapsed waiting and source UTC timestamps for the ordering comparison. The supplied excerpt has array indexes and timestamps, not native decision IDs or book hashes; do not imply otherwise.

## What the rule does and does not cause

The rule causally changes the program's action: it prevents immediate reservation on the eligible snapshot above and makes acceptance depend on later received information. Under the stated timestamp interpretation, any confirmed entry has a spot observation at least as late as the seed book and later than the seed spot. In this excerpt the episode cancels at 17:38:51.329, 1,139 ms after arming. No future observation is inserted into the original decision, so a prospective implementation has no look-ahead. A historical replay must preserve receive order and place any resulting decision at the later evaluation time.

It does **not** establish that fewer stale-information mistakes occur among the trades that remain, nor an empirical causal effect on profit. Timestamp ordering is an observable condition; information equivalence is not. Polymarket documents the book field as a snapshot timestamp, and exposes separate RTDS event and price-payload timestamps. These interfaces do not supply a cross-feed guarantee about shared clock accuracy or about all information reflected in a quote. [Official order-book schema](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/market-data/get-order-books-request-body), [official RTDS schemas](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-data/realtime-data#crypto-prices).

Primary market-microstructure work shows that public information and differences in reaction time can generate short-lived pricing discrepancies in continuous order books. That supports examining timing and explains why a genuine favorable quote can also vanish during a wait. Its empirical markets and theoretical assumptions do not validate this BTC conditional-probability model or this gate. This review's application to delayed spot selection is an inference. [Budish, Cramton and Shim (2015), author publication page and abstract](https://ericbudish.org/publication/the-high-frequency-trading-arms-race-frequent-batch-auctions-as-a-market-design-response/).

Concrete limitations and counterexamples:

- **Current book can still know more.** Seed: book time 10.000, spot time 09.000, floor 0.80, ask 0.70, eligible after allowances. Confirmation: spot time 10.000, book time 12.000, floor still 0.80, ask 0.60, all ages below five seconds. The book may have incorporated a BTC fall after 10.000 that the spot feed has yet to deliver. The gate passes and the same error recurs. The real excerpt's first watermark-crossing snapshot has a 2,592 ms current gap, although its ordinary strategy checks reject it.
- **A genuine brief opportunity can be lost.** A temporarily cheap ask may be removed before any qualifying spot arrives, even when the original model was directionally right. The gate can reduce fills or move entries to more expensive prices. Available depth and an observed ask do not prove a counterfactual fill.
- **Persistence can select the wrong errors.** Persistent model miscalibration or a continuing lead by market participants can survive confirmation, while real fleeting advantages disappear. The accepted subset can have a higher fraction of mistakes. Repeated attempts within a round are correlated and cannot be counted as independent outcomes.
- **Clock/source meaning is imperfect.** Source timestamps can be coarse or clocks offset; a reported price can already aggregate older observations. Crossing `b0` does not demonstrate that spot contains every information item in that book. Source-age bounds still matter; receiving a newer timestamp is not a universal freshness certificate.
- **Survivorship and tuning.** Looking only at confirmed episodes hides delays, missing-data cancellations and lost candidates. Choosing the rule after this one episode is hypothesis generation. Freeze its definition before collecting later evaluation data; do not tune a delay, age limit or confirmation count against this event.

Requiring spot time to reach the *current* book every time would be a different rule. It might sharply reduce availability or never pass under stable relative lag. Do not silently change the proposed rule into that moving requirement or claim the fixed-watermark version achieves it.

1. Replay this excerpt in recorded receive/snapshot order with faithful fixture inputs. Arm at 17:38:50.190; cancel at 17:38:51.329; assert zero reservation/sign/post calls. If testing the actual strategy evaluator, supply the real required history from the complete run or an explicitly synthetic history fixture. The 60-record excerpt alone cannot reconstruct its 1,800-second volatility windows.
2. Keep the same side independently eligible while new spots successively remain below, equal, and above `b0`; only the first qualifying later source point can confirm. Test the fractional-second case `49.000 < 49.189 <= 50.000`, duplicate snapshots, duplicate source points with changed receipt time, and out-of-order source events. When `s0 >= b0`, a later snapshot reusing `s0` must still wait under the strengthened rule.
3. Feed several newer books while spot catches up. Assert the original watermark is not moved; confirmed sizing/limit comes from the current book. Separately demonstrate the residual-lag counterexample above passes the timestamp gate while remaining subject to ordinary checks. This guards against false synchronization claims.
4. Invalidate surplus, spread, depth or price band before the watermark crossing, then restore eligibility. Assert the original episode stays canceled and the restored candidate must start fresh. Cover opposite-side selection and same-side re-arming.
5. Cover stale/missing snapshot, invalid timestamp, missing history, anchor conflict, condition/token/round/configuration change, and window exit. Test value's exactly 60 seconds remaining versus below 60 and momentum's exactly 90 versus below 90, plus an awaited prepare/preflight operation crossing each selected entry boundary. Same-price authoritative anchor verification must not be confused with a conflict.
6. Confirm once, then replay that snapshot. Assert at most one reservation/intent/POST through ordinary uniqueness controls. Cover reservation rejection, metadata invalidation, restart before confirmation, restart after reservation, unknown submission, stop, and risk/account blocks. Pending diagnostics must not consume cash or the opening-intent allowance.
7. Exercise confirmation and cancellation with each configured execution mode. Preserve stateless value/momentum comparison decisions and the predeclared calibration observation. Record gate state separately. Assert the non-selected raw comparison does not acquire the execution gate and exposure exits do not wait for it. A mode/configuration switch cancels pending confirmation; one mode cannot inherit the other's episode.

For the next frozen observation run, report all unique rounds and eligible episodes, how many waited/confirmed/canceled/missed data/left the window, time spent waiting, source/book gaps at seed and confirmation, and changes in model floor, current executable depth and costs. Report the ungated candidate decisions on the same receive-ordered observations. These are decision/availability measurements; do not turn them into hypothetical fills or profit. Keep the existing round-level probability calibration separate. Receipt-confirmed live outcomes, if later explicitly authorized, remain necessary to assess the complete execution policy; a single good or bad round cannot establish a stable return.
